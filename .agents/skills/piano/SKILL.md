# Piano practice notes

Turn the supplied lesson transcript into notes the pianist can glance at while practicing and immediately know what to do.

## Voice and layout

- Write in lowercase with short sentences and simple words.
- Sound like a smart caveman: light humor, useful advice, and no loss of accuracy.
- Keep musical terms exact. Explain unfamiliar terms briefly in plain words.
- Use bold labels and short bullets.
- Remove filler, unrelated chatter, repeated dialogue, and empty praise.
- Avoid long paragraphs and repeated points.

## Content

Include only sections supported by useful transcript content:

1. Start with **big takeaway:** followed by the single most important lesson in one bold sentence.
2. Under **scales and exercises**, list what the student worked on and any assigned changes, including hands together or separate, speed, octaves, and fingering. Tie fingering reminders and teacher feedback to the relevant exercise.
3. Under **main practice technique**, explain the central drill as numbered physical actions the student can perform at the piano. Say what the drill trains in one sentence. Make clear what to do slowly, what to do quickly, and when to pause.
4. Give each song its own bold label. State what needs work and exactly how to practice it. Preserve clear details about notes, chords, measures, fingering, hand movement, accompaniment, melody, pedal, rhythm, and mistakes. Explain specialized terms such as arpeggio briefly. Include concrete progress feedback when it helps.
5. End with **next practice:** and a short practice order based on the teacher's instructions. Include assigned amounts when stated.

## Accuracy

- Preserve the distinction between work already done and the teacher's next assignment.
- Fix only obvious transcription errors that context resolves.
- Briefly flag unclear details instead of guessing.
- Never invent times, repetition counts, fingering, notes, or assignments.
- Keep advice scoped to the exercise or passage where the teacher gave it.
- If the transcript ends before feedback or instructions, say so.
- Keep enough detail to practice correctly while making the result fast to scan.
