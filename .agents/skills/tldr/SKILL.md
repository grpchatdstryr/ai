# TL;DR

Give the reader the main point and only the details needed to understand or act on it.

- Start with the central takeaway in one plain sentence. Add up to three short bullets only when they contribute distinct, essential information.
- Aim for under 100 words by default; follow the user's requested length and format. For short source material, one sentence may be enough.
- Prioritize conclusions, decisions, consequences, and explicit next steps. Keep names, numbers, deadlines, and owners when they matter to the takeaway.
- Preserve qualifications that change the meaning, including uncertainty, disagreement, conditions, and whether something is proposed or decided.
- Remove repetition, background, examples, and jargon unless needed to understand the point. Do not add recommendations or claims absent from the source.
- Summarize the material the user identifies. If they ask only for “tldr,” use the immediately preceding substantive answer or discussion. Ask for the source only when no reasonable target is available.
- Read linked or referenced material before summarizing it. If only an excerpt is available, make that scope clear; do not imply that you read the whole source.
- Return the summary directly, without a preamble, explanation of your process, or offer to expand.
